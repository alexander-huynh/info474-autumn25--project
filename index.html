Not All Cars Pollute Equally
Every car on the road leaves a CO₂ trail behind it... but some leave a faint whisper, others a thick cloud. This article is an investigation: what actually drives those differences, and how does your car fit into the story?
Step 1 — Fuel Types: Where Emissions Begin
We start simple: fuel choice. Petrol, diesel. This section compares average CO₂ emissions by fuel type so you can see how much the choice at the pump (or charger) changes the climate cost of every mile.
Step 2 — Engine Size: Bigger Engines, Bigger Emissions
Fuel is only part of the story. Here, you explore how engine displacement shapes CO₂: as engines get larger, emissions climb. Use the controls to isolate different engine sizes and spot where the trend holds, and where outliers break it.
Step 3 — Horsepower: Performance vs. Environmental Cost
We love fast cars. The atmosphere doesn’t. This section links horsepower to CO₂ output, letting you highlight high-HP models or EVs to see how often performance and pollution travel together, and when technology bends that trade-off.
Step 4 — Manufacturer Personality: Who Builds Clean, Who Doesn’t?
Cars don’t choose their own emissions; manufacturers do. Here you compare brands by their average CO₂ across models. Clicking a manufacturer reveals whether their lineup leans dirty, clean, or somewhere in between.
Step 5 — Find Your Balance: Performance, Price, and CO₂
By now you’ve seen how fuel, size, and power all shape emissions. This section turns that knowledge into action: adjust sliders for CO₂, horsepower, price, and fuel type, and the recommender surfaces cars that match your values and constraints.
Step 6 — Search Your Own Car: Where Do You Stand?
Time to make it personal. Search for your current (or dream) car and see how it compares to thousands of other vehicles in the dataset. Is it cleaner than you expected, or dirtier than it looks on the lot?
What the Data Says About “Normal” Cars
Stepping back, the patterns are hard to ignore: fuel type, engine size, and horsepower matter a lot, and brand decisions matter too. Most cars cluster in a “normal” band of emissions, but there are clear ways to move above or below that line.
About This Dataset
The visuals in this article are built from EU vehicle registration and emissions records combined with car-spec datasets (engine size, horsepower, price, and fuel type). Values are simplified and aggregated for clarity, not to replace official regulatory reporting.
Where You Go From Here
This article can’t choose a car for you, but it can make the climate cost of that choice visible. Use what you’ve seen here to question “normal” cars, ask better questions at the dealership, or just look at the highway a little differently.
