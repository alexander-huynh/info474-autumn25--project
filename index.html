Not All Cars Pollute Equally
Every car leaves some CO₂ behind—but not all of them leave the same trail. Some sip fuel, others burn through it. This walkthrough breaks down why that happens, and where your own car fits in.
Step 1 — Fuel Types: Where Emissions Begin
First stop: fuel. Petrol, diesel; they don’t behave the same once you burn them. This view shows how much CO₂ comes from each type so you can see the impact of what goes into the tank.
Step 2 — Engine Size: Bigger Engines, Bigger Emissions
Fuel isn’t everything. Engine size plays a big role too. Bigger engines usually mean more CO₂. This section lets you zoom in on different engine sizes to see the pattern and the exceptions.
Step 3 — Horsepower: Performance vs. Environmental Cost
More power usually means more emissions. This view links horsepower to CO₂ so you can check out which high-HP cars stay efficient, and which ones trade speed for a bigger carbon footprint.
Step 4 — Manufacturer Personality: Who Builds Clean, Who Doesn’t?
Brands have habits. Some consistently build cleaner cars, others… not so much. This plot shows the average CO₂ of each manufacturer, and clicking a name highlights what their lineup looks like overall.
Step 5 — Find Your Balance: Performance, Price, and CO₂
By now you’ve seen the big factors. Here you can mix and match them yourself. Adjust CO₂, horsepower, price, and fuel type to see which cars match what you care about.
Step 6 — Search Your Own Car: Where Do You Stand?
Time to check your actual car. Type in the model and see how it stacks up against thousands of others. Maybe it’s cleaner than it feels… or maybe the numbers tell a different story.
What the Data Says About “Normal” Cars
When you pull back, the trends are pretty clear: fuel, engine size, horsepower, and brand choices add up. Most cars fall into a pretty predictable band, but there are definite ways to land above or below it.
About This Dataset
These visuals use EU vehicle registration and emissions data, paired with specs like engine size, horsepower, fuel type, and price. Values are simplified for clarity; they’re meant for exploration, not official reporting.
Where You Go From Here
This walkthrough won’t tell you what to buy. It just makes the climate cost of each option clearer. Take it with you when you’re comparing cars, or just use it to see the road a little differently.
